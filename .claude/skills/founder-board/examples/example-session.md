# Example session (fictional)

> Everything here is invented to show the mechanics: the product, the buyer and every number.
> It is not a real company and not advice for one.

**User:** *Run the board. Should we raise our price and add an annual plan, or expand into
adjacent verticals?*

---

## Brief (Chair)

- **Product:** a booking and reminders app for independent pet groomers (1–3 chairs).
- **Buyer:** the owner-operator, who books by phone and text today.
- **Model:** monthly subscription, one tier. ASSUMPTION: price is at the low end of the
  category.
- **Stage:** early paid traction, a few hundred paying shops (range given by user); churn is
  "noticeable in month 2" — no exact figure.
- **Options:** **A** raise the price and add a discounted annual plan · **B** expand to adjacent
  verticals (mobile vets, pet sitters) · **C** baseline: keep current course.
- **Constraints:** two-person team; no paid acquisition budget this quarter.
- **One-way door:** a price raise on existing customers is hard to undo.

**Weights:** Offers **3** (it is a pricing question) · Monopoly **2** (B is a market-scope
question) · Product **1** (neither option is mainly about the product).

---

## Round 1 (blind, parallel)

### Offers lens — Round 1

**Read of the brief:** the price is not anchored to the outcome (fewer no-shows), so the offer
is underpriced and month-2 churn suggests time-to-value is too slow.

| Option | Score | Why |
|--------|-------|-----|
| A | 7 | Annual plan pulls cash forward; price raise is justified if tied to no-show reduction |
| B | 3 | Splits the message before the core offer is proven |
| C | 3 | Leaves value on the table and does not address month-2 churn |

**Top option:** A · **What kills it:** existing shops churn on the raise before seeing the value
· **Ruin risk:** none · **One-way door:** yes, for existing customers · **Assumptions:**
ASSUMPTION reminders measurably cut no-shows · **Confidence:** low · **Question:** how many shops
reach their first reminder sent in week 1?

### Monopoly lens — Round 1

**Read of the brief:** the niche (solo groomers) is small enough to own; leaving it early trades
dominance for breadth.

| Option | Score | Why |
|--------|-------|-----|
| A | 7 | Deepens the niche; annual plans raise switching cost |
| B | 3 | Three small markets, dominance in none; no proprietary edge carries over |
| C | 4 | Keeps the niche but builds no moat |

**Top option:** A · **What kills it:** a general booking tool adds a groomer template ·
**Ruin risk:** none · **One-way door:** no · **Confidence:** medium · **Question:** what data do
you have that a general tool doesn't?

### Product lens — Round 1

**Read of the brief:** month-2 churn is a product signal before it is a pricing one.

| Option | Score | Why |
|--------|-------|-----|
| A | 6 | Fine if the first week gets fixed; a raise on a leaky experience amplifies churn |
| B | 2 | New verticals mean new flows; the opposite of focus |
| C | 5 | Gives room to fix onboarding |

**Top option:** A · **What kills it:** raising the price before setup is painless ·
**Ruin risk:** none · **One-way door:** no · **Confidence:** medium · **Question:** what does a
new shop have to do before the first reminder goes out?

---

## Chair between rounds

**UNANIMITY ALARM.** All three lenses top-ranked A, with scores 7, 7, 6 (within 1 point).
Strongest alternative nobody argued: **D — a paid "done-for-you setup" tier**: the team imports
the shop's client list and sends the first reminders, at a higher price. It addresses the
churn signal all three cards mention without raising the price on everyone.

**Cruxes**
1. Is month-2 churn caused by price/value or by setup effort? (Offers vs. Product)
2. Does an annual plan build durability, or just delay churn by a year? (Monopoly vs. Offers)
3. Must-answer: is D stronger than A? (all)

---

## Round 2 (debate) — summary of the three cards

| Option | Offers R1→R2 | Monopoly R1→R2 | Product R1→R2 |
|--------|--------------|----------------|---------------|
| A | 7 → 6 (concedes Product: raise before fixing setup amplifies churn) | 7 → 6 (concedes Offers: annual plan delays churn, doesn't cure it) | 6 → 6 |
| B | 3 → 3 | 3 → 2 | 2 → 2 |
| C | 3 → 3 | 4 → 4 | 5 → 5 |
| D | — → 7 (cuts effort and time-to-value, priced to outcome) | — → 6 (setup data becomes a switching cost) | — → 5 (concedes it works, rejects that a service hides a product gap) |

Ruin risk: none from all three. One-way door: D is reversible (Offers, Monopoly, Product: no).
Confidence: Offers low (no price test yet), Monopoly medium, Product medium.

---

## Verdict — raise price + annual plan vs. adjacent verticals

| Option | Offers (3) | Monopoly (2) | Product (1) | Weighted | Band |
|--------|-----------|--------------|-------------|----------|------|
| D — done-for-you setup tier | 7 | 6 | 5 | **6.3** | GO WITH CONDITIONS |
| A — price raise + annual | 6 | 6 | 6 | 6.0 | GO WITH CONDITIONS |
| C — baseline | 3 | 4 | 5 | 3.7 | DON'T DO IT |
| B — adjacent verticals | 3 | 2 | 2 | 2.5 | DON'T DO IT |

Arithmetic: D = (3×7 + 2×6 + 1×5) / 6 = 38 / 6 = 6.3.

**Decision:** GO WITH CONDITIONS — D, a paid done-for-you setup tier (6.3). No cap applied.
**Spread:** 2 · **Confidence:** medium (one lens at low confidence) · **Unanimity alarm:** raised;
Round 2 changed the ranking, so the agreement on A was tested and broken.
**Conditions:** offer D to new shops only; keep current pricing for existing shops for now;
fix the self-serve first week in parallel (Product's rejection stands).
**What would kill it:** shops won't pay for setup they believe should be free.
**Next test (≤ 2 weeks):** offer D on the signup page to new shops at a stated price; measure
take rate and week-1 activation vs. self-serve. **Kill criterion:** fewer than 1 in 10 new shops
take it, or activation is no better than self-serve.
