# Lens rubrics

Each lens applies its framework as a set of questions. Score each question mentally, then give
one 0–10 score per option. These are short summaries in our own words of published frameworks;
they are not quotations, and the lenses never speak as the authors.

---

## Offers lens

*Source framework: the value-equation approach to building offers (see README credits).*

Score the option by how much it raises the **value a buyer perceives relative to the price**.

| Question | Low (0–3) | High (8–10) |
|----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Dream outcome** — is the result the buyer actually wants named concretely? | Feature list, no outcome | One outcome the buyer would pay to have tomorrow |
| **Perceived likelihood** — why would they believe it works for *them*? | No proof, no guarantee | Proof from people like them, a guarantee that removes risk |
| **Time to value** — how fast is the first win? | Weeks of setup | Minutes or a first session |
| **Effort & sacrifice** — what must the buyer do or give up? | Migration, learning curve, habit change | Done-for-them or near-zero effort |
| **Price to value** — is the price anchored to the outcome, not to cost or competitors? | Cheapest in the category | Clearly worth many times the price |
| **Offer shape** — bonuses, guarantee, scarcity/urgency that are *true* | None, or fake urgency | Stack that removes every objection honestly |

Kill question: *Who is the buyer who would say yes today, and what would make them say no?*

## Monopoly lens

*Source framework: the monopoly-vs-competition approach to startups (see README credits).*

Score the option by how much it moves the business toward **owning a market** rather than
competing in one.

| Question | Low (0–3) | High (8–10) |
|----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Small market first** — is there a niche it can dominate soon? | "Everyone", or a crowded market | A small, reachable group it can own outright |
| **Proprietary edge** — is something ~10× better, not 10% better? | Parity with alternatives | An order-of-magnitude advantage on what matters |
| **Network effects** — does each user make it better for the next? | None | Strong, from the first niche |
| **Scale economics** — do unit costs fall as it grows? | Linear cost per customer | Mostly fixed cost, near-zero marginal |
| **Brand & durability** — will it still matter in 10 years? | Easy to copy, trend-driven | Hard to replicate, compounding |
| **Secret** — what does it know that most people don't believe? | Consensus view | A specific, defensible non-consensus truth |
| **Distribution** — is there a repeatable way to reach buyers? | "It will spread" | A named channel with known economics |

Kill question: *Who else could ship this next month, and why wouldn't they win?*

## Product lens

*Source framework: the focus-and-simplicity approach to product (see README credits).*

Score the option by how much it makes the product **focused, simple and complete** for one
specific person.

| Question | Low (0–3) | High (8–10) |
|----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Focus** — what is being said no to? | Many audiences, many features | One person, one job, the rest cut |
| **Simplicity** — is it simple because the problem was understood deeply? | Simple by omission, or complex | Obvious to use, nothing to explain |
| **End to end** — is the whole experience owned, from discovery to support? | Seams the user must stitch | Every step designed |
| **Details & taste** — does the part nobody sees get the same care? | Rough edges everywhere | Craft visible in small things |
| **Love test** — would a specific user be upset if it disappeared? | Indifferent | Would complain loudly, tell friends |

Kill question: *What would you remove so that what's left is great?*
